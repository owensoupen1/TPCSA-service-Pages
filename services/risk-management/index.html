What Does NPO Risk Management Involve?

Most NPO failures we see are not dramatic. A single person approves and pays. A grant is spent on the wrong cost line. A funder’s reporting deadline slips. NPO risk management is about spotting those weak points before a donor, auditor or the press does. We work with boards to build a risk register that reflects your programmes — not a corporate template — and to agree who owns each risk. If you want a quick view first, the Needs Analysis is a good starting point.

We then test the controls that matter: segregation of duties, bank and payment approvals, procurement, payroll, asset registers and donor-condition tracking. The output is a short, prioritised action list sized to your staff numbers and budget.

Our approach

Proportionate Controls, Not Paperwork for Its Own Sake

A ten-person community organisation and a multi-donor programme need different controls. We start by walking through how money actually moves — from donor agreement to bank to beneficiary — and map where it could go wrong. Controls are then matched to the risk and to the people you have.

For donor-funded organisations, including those with US government awards, we check that systems can meet funder rules on allowable costs, procurement and record keeping. Clean controls make the later NPO audit and review services faster and cheaper, whoever your auditor is.

Risk work often surfaces accounting or structure issues. Where it does, our NPO accountants and NPO legal structuring teams pick them up directly, and NPO training helps staff and boards apply new policies.

Frequently Asked Questions About NPO Risk Management

What is NPO risk management?

It is the board’s process for identifying what could harm the organisation — financial loss, fraud, non-compliance, reputational damage, programme failure — and deciding how to reduce it. In practice that means a risk register, sensible internal controls and regular reporting to the board.

Who is responsible for risk in an NPO?

The board, directors or trustees carry ultimate responsibility. Management runs the day-to-day controls. A risk register makes those responsibilities clear and gives the board something concrete to review.

We are small. Do we really need internal controls?

Yes, but scaled to you. Even with two finance staff you can separate who captures, who approves and who releases payments, and add board review of bank statements. Small organisations are often the most exposed because one person does everything.

How do you assess fraud risk?

We look at where cash, payments, procurement, payroll and stock could be misused, interview key staff and test a sample of transactions. You receive a short report of the main fraud risks and practical controls to reduce them.

Can you help us meet donor compliance requirements?

Yes. We review grant agreements against your systems — allowable costs, procurement thresholds, document retention and reporting — and identify gaps before a funder monitoring visit or audit.

Do you work with organisations receiving USAID or other US government funds?

Yes. Our audit director has experience with the US Government Auditing Standards (Yellow Book) and federal internal-control standards (Green Book). We can review readiness against those expectations.

Which policies should an NPO have?

Typical core policies cover finance and delegation of authority, procurement, conflicts of interest, fraud and whistleblowing, data protection under POPIA, and asset management. We prioritise based on your risks rather than handing over a generic pack.

Do NPOs have anti-money-laundering obligations?

Non-profits can be misused for money laundering and terrorist financing, and South Africa has strengthened oversight of the sector. Know-your-donor and know-your-beneficiary checks are good practice. Check the FIC and DSD guidance for current obligations.

Is a risk review the same as an audit?

No. An audit gives an opinion on financial statements. A risk review looks forward at where things could go wrong and how to fix controls. Good risk work makes audits smoother.
